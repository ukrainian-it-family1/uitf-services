## Block 1. The problem

**H2:** Sound familiar?

- **The process lives in 4 places.** The schedule in one tool, invoices in another, partner payouts in a spreadsheet, the rest in someone's inbox.
- **A complaint means a recalculation.** A discount for the client, a new share for the partner, a new margin for you. All by hand.
- **Month end takes a week.** And at the end of it nobody can explain why the figures differ.
- **One person holds it together.** The whole process sits in their head. If they leave, so does the process.

**Closing line:** None of this is a software problem yet. It becomes one the day you open the next city.

## Block 3. Main case

**Eyebrow:** Case study · Property services · Active since 2023
**H2:** Andulka: new cities, no new back office.

**Text:**
A property service business that grows across cities and countries through partners. Before the platform, invoicing, partner payouts and complaints lived in spreadsheets and bank statements, reconciled by hand. Every mistake cost money directly: a wrong payout, an unaccounted complaint, a wrong discount.

We built one system on a strict hierarchy: country, city, building, entrance. The schedule drives the charges. From it the system derives the client invoice, the partner payout and the service revenue. Complaints adjust discounts, partner income and margin automatically. DAC7 exports come from the same data.

**Facts:**

| 3+ years | 4 levels | 1 source |
|---|---|---|
| active engagement, still running | country, city, building, entrance | the schedule drives invoice, payout and revenue |

## Block 8. FAQ

**H2:** Questions owners ask us.

**We tried this before. Nobody used the system.**
That usually happens when a system is built around how it looks, not how people work. We design for daily use: dense screens, filters that follow your process, the fewest clicks on the work your team repeats every day.

**Our process isn't standard.**
That's the reason to build. If it were standard, a template would do, and we'd tell you to use one.

**What will it cost?**
That's what the 48 hour estimate answers, phase by phase, before you commit to anything. Phase one has a fixed scope and a fixed price.

**Do we need a technical person on our side?**
No. You need someone who knows the process. The technical decisions are our job.

**Can we start small?**
Yes. Phase one is deliberately small: the part of the process that costs you most today.

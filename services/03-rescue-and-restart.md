## Block 1. When to call us

**H2:** This page is for you if:

- **A contractor left.** And nobody can explain how the system works or why it was built this way.
- **You hold a rewrite quote.** It's a big number, and you want a second opinion before you sign.
- **Real users broke the demo.** It worked in the presentation. It doesn't work under real load.
- **The project stopped halfway.** You need to know whether it can restart, or whether you're paying to finish the wrong thing.

## Block 2. The argument

**H2:** The one purchase that can end with "don't".

**Text:**
A rewrite commits you to spending more. So does a refactor. An audit is the only thing you can buy where the best outcome is keeping your money.

Most contractors recommend the rewrite, because it's the biggest contract on the shelf. We're fine telling you to do less.

**Story card:**
> A client came to us with a six figure quote for a full rewrite. After the audit, the part they wanted to rewrite turned out to be fine. The money stayed with the client.

## Block 7. FAQ

**H2:** Questions owners ask us.

**You'll tell me to rewrite everything anyway.**
A rewrite would be the bigger contract for us. That's exactly why the audit has to be able to end with "don't". If the code is fine, you'll hear it.

**I'm not technical. Will I understand the result?**
Yes. Every item comes with what it means for your business and what it costs. No scores, no jargon.

**Do you need the previous contractor?**
No. Access to the code and hosting is enough. If someone who worked on it is available, 30 minutes with them saves time.

**What if the answer is a rebuild?**
Then you spend with confidence instead of hope. The point was never to avoid the rebuild. It was to avoid buying it blind.
